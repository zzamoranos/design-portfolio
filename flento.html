Flento · UX Design Internship

Simplifying client management for fitness trainers

Redesigned how trainers add, understand and schedule clients in Flento’s CRM, grounded in competitive research, a wireframe audit and usability testing.

Role
UX Design Intern
Timeline
Jan – May 2024
Skills

Product Design

Overview

Flento is Voiceflip’s all-in-one business management platform for gyms, studios and independent trainers.

As one of the UX design interns on the team, I focused on redesigning the experience for client management and the scheduling flows for new clients.

Understanding

Discovering the space

. ܁₊ ⊹ . ܁˖ .

The problem

Once clients were added, trainers didn’t know what to do next.

Adding clients worked, but everything after felt unclear. Home was a set of shortcuts, the clients page was a contact list, and the data trainers logged about each client stayed buried in their contact card.

This left trainers questioning:

  • What do I need to do today?
  • What does my client need from me?
  • How do I manage a connection long-term?

Diving deeper

Research and process

. ܁₊ ⊹ . ܁˖ .

Audit

Personal first impressions

Before designing, I audited the existing wireframes in Figma and tested the core flows.

Client setup ended at a basic contact form (name, email, phone), and the new client then appeared in a general list. Nothing signalled that a detailed client view existed.

  1. Insight 01

    Home solved for setup only

    An existing user opening Flento needs to quickly see upcoming appointments and tasks to act on. Instead, they’re met with a grid of set-up shortcuts.

  2. Insight 02

    Client data is hidden

    Progress and details are hidden within contact cards. Research shows clients are 35% more likely to cancel within six months when progress isn’t clear (IHRSA, 2024).

  3. Insight 03

    Unclear engagement tracking

    Flento had no signal for engagement (e.g. missed sessions or lapsed bookings), so trainers couldn’t spot a client losing momentum without relying on memory.

Diving into UXR…

Three main discovery pillars for the research approach

  • Prioritization

    What are the key features or functionalities that trainers prioritize most to ensure high customer satisfaction and effectively manage their administrative tasks?

  • Business strategies

    How do trainers build effective business strategies from data insights on their clients to improve client retention? What key features support business growth, operational efficiency, and lower churn rates?

  • Habits

    Understanding trainers’ habits and interactions with the CRM system, to learn their daily workflows and optimize usability and efficiency.

Key findings

To guide research on how we could improve client management in Flento, I identified five components of an ideal human relationship:

  • Shared feeling of being understood & accepted

  • Mutual trust & respect

  • Connection & belonging

  • Good communication & problem solving

  • Stability & shared values

Clients stay when they feel seen, and leave when they don’t.

Data showed that clients break up with their local gym or trainer typically around the five-month mark. Clients drift and disengage when they stop seeing progress, when sessions feel generic and impersonal, or when communication and trust falter. By the time a trainer notices, many have already lost the spark, or left within their first few months.

The clients who stay long-term see continuous growth and progress, get training that feels built for them, and feel supported in their fitness journey even outside of sessions.

Client–trainer relationships drift because of poor communication, a lack of support, and the challenge of running a fitness business smoothly. According to IHRSA, recruiting a new member is 5x more expensive than keeping an existing one, so it was important to look at how Flento could reduce churn from both a client-to-trainer (DTC) and B2B perspective.

Persona

Using what we knew about our current and target users, I created a persona to guide research and design explorations:

User persona

The Beginner Trainer

About
  • Trainers are mostly (90% of users) middle-aged, entrepreneurial, retired and traditional. 10% are Gen Z to millennial, and tech-savvy.
  • Trainers find it hard to manage their fitness admin efficiently and affordably, while also working to retain their clients.
Motivations & goals
  • Streamline business operations to focus more on training clients and maintaining strong client relationships.
  • A platform that handles all aspects of client management in one place, like automated invoicing, payment processing and communication.
Common problems
  • Hard to manage clients’ appointments, payments and communication while also focusing on providing quality training.
  • Coordinating with clients and tracking their progress is difficult without a central system.
  • Struggles with manual invoicing and payment processing, which take time and energy.

Between Instagram, email, WhatsApp, texts, and notes and schedules kept in different places, information gets lost and messages go unread. Flento’s in-app communication and client management features were meant to solve this. But was the existing solution effective?

Organization & efficiency

Trainers want efficient processes for managing client profiles, measuring service quality and satisfaction, and accessing client information, messages and documents.

Increase retention & engagement

Clients can feel hesitant about training, especially remotely, believing they won’t get the same results without a coach there. Without regular check-ins and clear progress, their doubts turn into drop-off.

Without central systems and efficient management, keeping clients engaged took time and effort.

Trainers need a simple way to track progress and stay present between sessions, and to make online coaching approachable, reliable and enjoyable.

What does this boil down to?

Flento’s CRM kept client data hidden, and gave no clear insight a business could act on to improve retention and oversee operations. Building on the existing system, trainers could keep adding clients, updating details and progress, and scheduling sessions in one place, with a dashboard showing which clients are progressing and which need attention.

To guide the designs, I set out to solve this problem:

How might we…

Optimize client management to help trainers spot clients losing momentum before anyone drops off?

Solution

Final designs

. ܁₊ ⊹ . ܁˖ .

Redesigned home and navigation

The new home leads with a clear view of what needs attention, so a trainer opening Flento sees their day at a glance. Only what’s relevant to the day is shown, so the screen never feels overwhelming.

A central “create” (+) button in the navigation opens quick actions: schedule a session, add a client, request a payment, send a message, or jump to a recently viewed client.

Omnichannel client management

Clients and messages now live in one tab, grouped into Active, New, Leads and Engagement.
